# Tai chi mark: AI video generation prompt

Distilled from `2026-08-02-markdown-tai-chi-brief.md` and the working study at `tai-chi.html`, for feeding to an AI video generator as an alternative or companion to the hand-coded SVG/JS animation. Not constrained by HTML animation feasibility, so it can go further on flow and visual clarity than the prototype.

## Why a video track at all

Feedback on the SVG study (version 6, "press and push" / An) was that the gesture reads as too subtle, especially once translated into Markdown-punctuation strokes — it doesn't obviously signal "movement" at small size or a glance. This prompt swaps the subtle press for a bigger, unmistakable sweeping arc while keeping everything else the brief established: side-on ink figure, grounded weight transfer, dissolving pose trail, later resolution into Markdown syntax.

## The prompt

> A minimalist ink-brush figure performs a slow, graceful tai chi movement in strict side profile, like a Muybridge motion study crossed with a Zen brush sketch. Pure black brush strokes on a warm paper-cream background — no fill, no face detail, no costume, just tapered calligraphic lines for spine, arms, and legs. The figure executes one large, unmistakable sweeping gesture: weight rolls back onto the rear leg, both arms draw in and gather low, then weight surges forward as both arms sweep out and up in a wide, expansive arc — a big, legible push/parry motion, not a subtle hand press. Motion is slow, grounded, and continuous, with visible weight transfer through the hips and legs, never a walk or dance step. As the figure moves, the last 2–3 poses linger behind it like fading ink, dissolving continuously — a sketchbook trail of the movement, not motion blur or speed lines. Midway through the loop, the brushstrokes dissolve into flying Markdown punctuation — `#`, `*`, `_`, `>`, backticks, brackets — which gusts in on wind to assemble the pose, holds, then scatters off in the direction of the final gesture, before reforming as ink again. Calm, meditative pacing; one full breath-length loop, 6–10 seconds; wide, shallow aspect ratio (like a horizontal banner, figure small relative to empty space); no camera movement, no 3D depth, no background scenery.

## What changed vs. the brief/study, and why

- **Gesture swapped**: brief's *An* (press and push, both palms drawing in then pressing out) replaced with a wide sweeping arc. Reason: direct feedback that the movement was too subtle to read, especially in punctuation form. Everything else — side profile, ink brush line quality, weight transfer through hips/legs, no walk/dance cycle, quiet head — carries over unchanged from the brief.
- **Everything else preserved**: strict side-on (Muybridge), no 3D, brush-stroke not stick-figure, dissolving pose trail (sketchbook not motion blur), Markdown-punctuation transformation as the second-stage treatment, small-scale/empty-space composition.

## Variants worth trying

- **Tighter single-pose prompt**: for image-to-video tools, describe just the gathered pose and the sweep-out as one motion, dropping the loop/scatter framing.
- **Ink-only cut**: drop the Markdown-punctuation paragraph entirely for a pure ink-figure video, useful for the "human form and motion first" gate the brief insists on before judging the syntax treatment.
