Draft 1 was three shades of one idea with a dead palette. Draft 2 fixes the story first, then explores wide.

Rufus's verdict on draft 1: the colour was off and boring, the directions didn't inspire, and the feel wasn't clear enough to design from. He is right on all three. This draft starts over from the narrative: what the site is, what the hero says, what it should feel like. Then six directions that are actually different from each other, each shown as a full hero in its own type and colour. The mark and the plan stay at the end.

The brief's own chrome is neutral on purpose so no direction borrows warmth from the page around it.

What this site is

Way Into AI found its one sentence ("use AI to understand it, understand it to use it well"). This site's equivalent, from Rufus's notes today: the format unlocks an ecosystem. Markdown matters because of what grows on it. The site is not another guide to markdown; it is the guide to the markdown ecosystem and how to do your work on it.

manifesto · why · vision

Markdown is eating the world

The evangelist thread. Why open formats matter; file over app; why a common format lets tools and people connect; where this is going. The manifesto, why.md, the ecosystem map.

the way · centre of the site

Do your work on markdown

The real curriculum. Replace Notion, run your docs, publish a site, keep a garden, build a catalog, all from files you own. Guides by use case, tutorials, the roadmap, markdown in every app.

mechanics · how it works

How formats work

The Making Software thread, new today. What a text format is, why it sits between storage and tools, how markdown builds up from blocks, why it is extensible, where it stops. The basics page grows into this.

Rufus's sketch, said in words: easy to publish, control your content, grows when you need to. Nothing sat in the middle of those three circles. Markdown-based work does. That triad is the promise; the three threads above are how the site delivers it.

The hero

"Markdown is a great format. What matters is what it lets you build, and that you own it afterwards" is a paragraph, not a headline. The hero needs five words or fewer that a reader repeats to someone else. Candidates, set large so they can be judged as headlines. The subline under each is a first draft in Rufus's register.

Markdown is eating the world.

One plain-text format now runs your notes, your docs, your website and every AI chat. Here's the way to do your work on it, and why that's a good idea.

Pick. Rufus's own line, already in the manifesto. Borrows a frame everyone knows, which is the point: it makes a claim, and the site is the "how and why" that follows. "Own the source" moves to the manifesto and the footer, where it works as a seal line rather than a slogan.

Plain text with superpowers.

Headings, links, tables, diagrams, whole databases: all in files that open anywhere and will still open in fifty years.

From the January vision doc. Explains the mechanics in four words. Friendly, slightly geeky, no enemy. Weaker as a flag than "eating the world"; stronger as the basics-section banner.

One format. Every tool.

Write once in markdown and use Obsidian, GitHub, Google Docs, your AI and your website on the same file.

The ecosystem claim in five words. Clean, but reads like a product feature list and could belong to any interoperability company.

Own the source.

Your notes, docs and sites as plain files. Use any tool. Leave any tool.

Current line, first half only. Strong and ownable. Says the why but not the what; a newcomer does not know what "source" is. Keep as the manifesto's closing line.

Easy to publish. Yours to keep. Grows with you.

Markdown is the one format that gives you all three. Here's how to use it for everything.

The Venn sketch as a line. True and complete, but a triad reads as marketing and it is nine words.

Recommended hero stack: headline "Markdown is eating the world." Subline in Rufus's voice, two sentences, the second starting "Here's the way". One primary call to action (the roadmap), one secondary (ten-minute basics). The tagline question from draft 1 dissolves: the hero does not need a two-part slogan.

Feel

Fun and cool and accessible, the way Notion is. Slightly geekier than that, the way Wikipedia is geekier than Britannica, and for the same reason: it is open and free, and much of its ecosystem is too. Elegant enough that a designer trusts it, plain enough that a non-technical reader relaxes. The calm of a practice; the energy of a thesis.

Is

confident, warm, explanatory, a little playful, visibly made by a person, open

Is not

a terminal, a SaaS landing page, developer docs, a monastery, a startup pitch, AI-generated

Closest cousins

Making Software for elegance and explanation; the Wikimedia illustrations for fun; Way Into AI for discipline and spacing

Colour note

Draft 1's cream and vermilion read as dead next to the current site's green. Whatever wins needs colour with some life in it, used generously in the illustrations and sparingly in the UI.

Mood board

Rufus's references first, then the draft 1 set that survives. Each with what to take and what to leave.

Six directions

Each is a full hero in its own type and colour with the recommended headline, so the comparison is visual. They are deliberately far apart. Two or three should be killed on sight; that is what they are for. The rest get developed into full page mocks.

1. The manual develop

Making Software's register, with our own masthead. Serif body (Source Serif 4), mono labels in caps, cobalt line diagrams on a dotted grid, figure numbers. Everything is explained by a drawing.

Feel check

Elegant, trustworthy, explanatory. The "how formats work" thread looks native here, and the ecosystem map becomes FIG.002. Less fun than the Wikimedia aim; the diagrams would need a few people in them.

Risk

Reads as a book, and the site is a place to do things. Mono is a supporting face, not the lead. Close to its reference; the masthead and illustration subject would have to carry the difference.

2. The field guide develop

The Wikimedia language made ours. Warm grey, flat fills, ink outlines, four colours (indigo, orange, green, red), small people doing things. Plus Jakarta Sans, heavy headings, pill buttons. Mono only in code.

Feel check

The most fun and the most accessible of the six. Notion-friendly without the SaaS gloss, because the illustration is hand-made and the colours are print colours. Open and generous, like the reference.

Risk

Leaves the monospace instinct behind entirely. Lives or dies on the illustration quality; the mock's drawing is a stand-in, and the real panels would be a week of drawing. The green here is a different green from today's.

3. The dojo

The tai chi idea given room. Instrument Serif at poster size, Geist Mono for everything small, warm paper, black ink, one vermilion seal. Huge figure, lots of air. The mark is the whole hero.

Feel check

Calm, premium, memorable. The attitude ("calm body, cheeky edge") in its purest form. Draft 1 tried to do this and did it timidly; this is what it looks like when it commits.

Risk

Not fun, and not accessible in the Notion sense. The naming doc warned the "Way" can drift into mysticism; the 道 is there to show where the line is. Vermilion already got a thumbs-down in draft 1.

4. The poster

Manifesto energy as a look. Bricolage Grotesque at 130 px, black on a yellow that is not quite a highlighter, raw syntax as texture, JetBrains Mono for all else. The punk teenager before the yuppie suit.

Feel check

Fun, loud, impossible to forget. Says "eating the world" like it means it. Cool in the way a record sleeve is cool.

Risk

Contradicts "calm body" directly; this is kung fu, not tai chi. Yellow pages are hard to read eighty guides on; the inner pages would have to go white and keep only the type. Dates fast.

5. The notebook develop

Rufus's sketch as the whole visual language. Ruled paper, IBM Plex Mono body, highlighter on the headline, hand-lettered annotations in red pencil, wobbly hand-drawn diagrams. The site as a working notebook, which is what it is.

Feel check

Fun, personal, visibly made by a person, geeky in the Wikipedia way. Monospace earns its place as "typed", not "terminal". Works in the open, like the voice does. Diagrams are cheap to make and on-brand when rough.

Risk

Handwriting fonts are a cliché when overused; the annotations must stay rare. The ruled background cannot run under long prose. Could read as unfinished rather than in-progress if the type is not tight.

6. The editorial

The Way Into AI formula, honestly applied with our own faces and colours. IBM Plex Mono for structure and the `#` markers, Fraunces for the voice, teal instead of blue, a mustard highlight. The raw/rendered pair is the hero device.

Feel check

Has the personality and spacing Rufus liked in WAI, and the pairing (mono structure, serif voice) that draft 1 threw away. Quietly confident; more editorial than fun.

Risk

It is the sibling in a different jumper. A visitor who knows both sites sees one studio. Fine for a family; not a new identity.

My read: the fun aim points at 2 or 5; the elegance aim at 1 or 6; the attitude at 3. The most interesting site is a hybrid of 5 and 1: a working notebook with the manual's discipline, where the rough sketches are how ideas arrive and the cobalt diagrams are how they get explained, and the field guide's flat illustrations appear at the top of each use-case guide. That is also the only combination that gives all three threads (manifesto, the way, mechanics) their own visual register inside one system. Develop 1, 2 and 5 into full pages; kill 3, 4 and 6 unless one of them surprises you.

The mark, briefly

Unchanged from draft 1 except that its colour now follows the direction. The syntax tai chi figure stays the hero animation, rebuilt in SVG with the push-hands beat: the figure settles, a plain block slides in, the figure yields and presses, the block leaves and dissolves into `#` `*` `>`. The static ink figure on a square of the accent colour is the small mark for navbar, favicon and social. In direction 2 the figure would be redrawn in the flat Wikimedia style; in 5 it would be inked by hand; in 1 it becomes FIG.000.

Plan

  1. Decide the headline. Recommended "Markdown is eating the world." Subline gets written properly in Rufus's voice once chosen.
  2. Pick two or three directions to develop. Recommended 1, 2, 5. Each becomes a full homepage plus one guide page, rendered at desktop and phone, with real copy.
  3. Pick one. Then the system goes into custom.css, the mark is rebuilt in that language, and the illustration or diagram style is set with two real examples.
  4. Content that the brand surfaced. Two beads filed today: the markdown ecosystem map and the "how formats work" section. Both are content, not brand, and both would be drawn in the chosen style.
