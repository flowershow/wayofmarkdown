Learn it

A hash for a heading, stars for bold, a dash for a list. That's most of it, and AI already writes it for you. Then: tables, links between notes, fields on a file, HTML when you need it.

It's already in more places than you've noticed: Google Docs, Obsidian, GitHub, Slack, every AI chat.

The basicsMarkdown in every app

Why

Nobody cares about formats, any more than they care about TCP/IP. You care about what they let you do. Markdown is the rare case where the format made the difference, and it wasn't a straight line.

People could write it by hand, so tools took it as their format. Tools took it, so more people wrote it. Twenty years of that, and the tools got good. AI arrived late and speaks it by default.

That's the whole philosophy: simple pieces you own beat one monolith you rent.

Replacing Notion with Markdown

How to move docs, wikis and light databases out of Notion into plain files you own, and what you give up.

Rufus Pollock · updated 2026 · 8 min read

Still weighing it up? The feature-by-feature scorecard is at Notion vs Markdown; this page is the how, for when you've decided.

Let me be clear upfront: Notion is genuinely good software. The blocks editor is slick, the databases are clever, and teams get real value from it. If it's working for you and the trade-offs below don't bother you, carry on.

But Notion is also a proprietary blocks database that owns your notes, your wiki and your little databases, rented back to you monthly. Your "pages" aren't files; they're rows in someone else's system. And most people don't realise that most of what they use Notion for works in plain markdown files. Not with heroic effort. With a nice editor and a couple of conventions.

The mapping

Take the things you actually do in Notion, one at a time:

In NotionIn markdownWith
Docs and notesa .md fileObsidian over a folder you own: live preview, slash commands, the lot.
Wikis[[wiki-links]]Obsidian for backlinks and graph; add GitHub for review and history.
Databasesfrontmatter fieldsA folder of files is a table. Obsidian Bases gives views, filters, cards.
Public pagespublish the folderFlowershow or any static site tool. A real site on your domain.

Databases are the interesting one, and the reason people think they're stuck. A Notion database is records with fields and views. But a markdown file with frontmatter is a record with fields:

---
title: The Dispossessed
author: Ursula K. Le Guin
status: read
rating: 5
---
Anarchist moon physics. Loved it.

What you give up, honestly

Not a free lunch

  1. Real-time multiplayer editing. Git-based collaboration is asynchronous: commit, push, review. Fine for docs and knowledge; wrong for six people live-editing one meeting note.
  2. Granular permissions. A repo is shared or it isn't.
  3. Heavy relational databases. Thousands of records and rollups want an actual database. (Though ask yourself honestly whether you have that, or four lists and a books catalogue.)

If those three are your core use, stay on Notion, sincerely. If, like most people, what you have is notes, docs, links and lists: you can own all of it.

The escape, step by step

  1. Export. Notion Settings → Export all workspace content → Markdown & CSV.
  2. Tidy. Notion appends ugly ID hashes to filenames; a rename pass fixes it.
  3. Open in Obsidian. Point it at the folder. That's it, you're operational.
  4. Add git when ready. Push the vault to GitHub for history, sync and collaboration.
  5. Publish if you want. Tutorial.

That's the actual point. Not that Notion is bad, but that your content shouldn't need an export button.
